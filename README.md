# Pokémon Draft League

A simple HTML, CSS, and JavaScript NFL draft game with two teams, Gen 1, Gen 2, and combined Pokémon pools. No app framework, account, or backend required.

Open `dist/index.html` on a computer, or serve the `dist` folder with any static web server. For example:

```sh
python -m http.server 8080 --directory dist
```

Open the published site in Safari on iPhone. Tap a position, then a Pokémon. Switch between Team 1 and Team 2 using the compact header tabs. Both teams use the same generation pool, and a Pokémon can occupy only one position across both teams. Picking one already on the current team swaps the two players; choosing it for an empty position moves it there. Pokémon on the other team are marked and unavailable. Changing personnel preserves inactive picks for when you switch back; those picks still reserve their Pokémon.

Use the hamburger menu for the selected team's name, generation, offense/defense personnel, export, link sharing, sample/clear, Hall of Fame, stats guide, and Safari screen-space guidance. Team names support 24 Unicode characters and appear in tabs, exports, links, and results. The menu starts closed on each load; the header shows each team's active pick count and the Simulate Match button. Personnel, Sample lineup, and Clear board affect the selected team. Retro art has been removed; existing saves and links use modern artwork while preserving their lineups.

The board uses a textured grass field with gently converging sidelines. Pokémon and labels stay upright, selection rings stay inside each tile, and narrow screens stagger tight ends to keep dense personnel groups readable. The live board and PNG export use the same perspective geometry.

Each generation mode remembers both teams' personnel and lineups in browser storage. Team 2 starts empty; Sample lineup fills the selected team with an example using Pokémon that remain available. Gen 2 uses Johto examples; combined mode starts Team 1 with Kanto and Team 2 with Johto. Legendary and mythical Pokémon are included. Existing single-team saves migrate to Team 1 with an empty Team 2.

Auction draft in the hamburger menu starts a separate matchup with $1,000 per team. Choose team names and a generation pool, then bid whole dollars on 44 unique random Pokémon, revealed one at a time. Only the winning team pays. Ties, including two $0 bids, are settled randomly among teams with room left. After each award, tap an empty field position on the winning team's board; Next Pokémon opens the next round. A team with 22 players stops bidding. Both teams finish with 22 players, ready for the usual match simulation and career records. Auction picks can swap positions with other Pokémon their team owns; formation changes move any displaced players into open active positions, preserving ownership. Sample, clear, and generation changes are disabled for auction teams.

Auction budgets, bids, both rosters, and pending placements save on this device. Regular draft restores the original lineups; Resume auction brings back the auction. New auction replaces only the saved auction after Start auction is pressed. Retired Pokémon are excluded; fewer than 44 eligible Pokémon blocks a new auction. Retiring an already purchased auction player ends that auction and restores regular drafts; retiring an unrevealed player substitutes another eligible prospect. Share links carry the two lineups as ordinary shared teams, without auction budgets or future prospects. Bidding is designed for two people using the same screen.

Export image prepares a 1600×1550 PNG of the selected team's 22 active positions, names, scouting stats, personnel, and generation. Share image opens native file sharing where supported; Save image is the fallback. Share link includes both team names and exact lineups, including inactive picks, and the selected team. Version 3 links use UTF-8 for non-Latin names and emoji. Shared matchups are saved separately until you choose Keep matchup, so opening a friend's link preserves your own teams. My teams returns to your original pair. Old single-team and unnamed two-team links still work. Storage failures do not prevent gameplay; keep a share link if the browser cannot save. Share links do not transfer career records or Hall of Fame membership.

Fill all 22 active positions on each team to enable Simulate Match. The game plays four quarters with twelve possessions per team, actual downs, field position, individual pass/run matchups, and personnel-dependent tendencies. Each receiver faces coverage; linemen oppose rushers and open running lanes; safeties and linebackers contribute to reads, tackling and pursuit. Positional grades dominate, while bounded lore factors and random play outcomes allow surprises. All 44 players receive an authoritative box score, including linemen's fantasy blocking/pressure stats, plus each team's biggest play and global offensive/defensive MVPs. Tap a player row for detailed stats. Passing yards equal receiving yards, targets equal pass attempts, turnovers and sack losses reconcile with the opposing defense, and team total yardage subtracts sack losses. Quarter points sum to the final score. Touchdowns include successful extra points, field goals count three, and games tied after four quarters finish as draws. Field goals are team events because no kicker is drafted. Play again runs a fresh game and adds one new career appearance per player.

All 251 species have individually reviewed physical traits, lore adaptations and position-fit anchors, producing 6,024 grades across 24 football roles. The model considers sourced height/weight and estimated 40-yard time, anatomy, moves, abilities, mobility, awareness and specific football mechanics. Creative powers are permitted: Alakazam's teleporting anticipation aids safety play, Blastoise's cannons launch passes, and Machamp's four arms combine blocking and catching. Shifty rushers receive credit for movement, burst and counter-moves; receivers without a feasible catch mechanism are heavily penalized. Canonical inspiration and fictional football adaptations are distinguished in `position-grade-design.json`. Grades stay out of drafting, exported boards, shared links and game results. Only an inducted Pokémon's Hall of Fame scouting report reveals its positional values and role explanations. This is a front-end game, so its JavaScript remains inspectable.

The Hall of Fame and career records belong to this browser's saved game. Career totals begin with matches played using this version, accumulate across teams and generation modes, and record each match ID once. Existing drafts migrate without invented historical match stats. Inducting any of the 251 Pokémon snapshots its career, stops its accumulation, and removes it from every saved/current/shared roster, including inactive formation slots. The picker, draft API, sample lineups, restored saves and imported links enforce retirement. Remove an inductee to make it available again; its prior lifetime stats remain and future matches resume accumulation. Cross-tab changes refresh eligibility. Blocked browser storage falls back to session records and displays an unsaved notice. Drafts retain the original storage key; careers use the separate `pokemon-draft-careers-v1` key. A Hall of Fame in another browser or device is independent.

Every Pokémon card shows height in feet/inches, weight in pounds, and an estimated 40-yard dash time in seconds. Height and weight were extracted from Bulbapedia's height/weight lists for IDs1–251, standard forms only, and verified against both lists and existing PokéAPI metadata. Original meters/kilograms remain in the catalog; display height rounds to the nearest inch and pounds to one decimal place. Very long Pokémon may use Pokédex body length as height. The stats guide links both sources.

40-yard times are manually authored fantasy scouting estimates based on movement anatomy, agility, and familiar lore, not measured results or conversions of battle Speed. The displayed dash policy uses a standing start on soft turf, permits ordinary flight/hovering/burrowing, and excludes teleportation, transformations, special launch boosts, and outside assistance. Positional grading separately permits those powers for creative football fit; it does not alter the displayed dash estimate. Aquatic Pokémon use their on-turf locomotion. All estimates have brief rationales in `pokemon-speed-estimates.json`; factual measurement provenance is in `pokemon-stats-sources.json`. Estimates are labeled in both the picker and exported boards.

More room explains Safari's Hide Toolbar option and adding the game to the Home Screen. The manifest and Apple web-app metadata support a standalone app view. A fullscreen button appears only in browsers that support it. Safari's toolbar and iOS share sheet still require user action. HTTPS is needed for native sharing and clipboard access; local file play supports drafting but may limit these browser features.

The 251 Pokémon records and transparent 475×475 artwork are bundled locally; the game does not call an external API. Barlow fonts are also bundled locally with system fallbacks and their open font license.

Artwork and data sources are recorded in `artwork-sources.json` and `gen2-artwork-sources.json`; the new asset checks are in `gen2-asset-verification.json`. Pokémon artwork belongs to The Pokémon Company; this is a fan project.

Field texture: [ambientCG Grass005](https://ambientcg.com/view?id=Grass005), used under [CC0 1.0](https://docs.ambientcg.com/license/). The local `dist/assets/turf.jpg` is the original 1024×1024 color texture.
